The first compact digital camera was created by Fujifilm in 1988. Called the FUJIX DS-1P, it took photos using a 400 kilopixel CCD and became the first camera that was fully digital. Its way of storing images was its most revolutionary feature; instead of storing data through a video floppy like other electronic cameras, it did so through a semiconductor memory card. At first, it was a challenge, as semiconductor memory cards were notably more expensive than video floppies. However, Fuji was unsatisfied with the poor data capacity of the older models, so it was determined to develop a camera that stored data through a memory card. As a result, the DS-1P came to be.
