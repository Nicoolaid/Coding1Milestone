The first polaroid camera, named the Model 95, was invented in 1948. Its creator, Edwin H. Land, first introduced his creation to the public through a meeting in New York City. At the time, the camera only produced sepia-toned photographs. In order to view a photo, the photographer had to wait 60 seconds before peeling off the top film to fully see the image. Once this camera hit the market, it became a hit. People were amazed at the ability to produce an image almost instantly. Although the device required tedious handling from the photographers, its system went to become the first stepping stone to instant photography.
